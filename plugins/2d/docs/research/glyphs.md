# Glyphs: bitmaps, SDF, MSDF or MTSDF, and shaping in Go

Research for [#862](https://github.com/dvoyni/cog/issues/862), on the map [#860](https://github.com/dvoyni/cog/issues/860). It asks which glyph representation 2D's text should use: bitmaps rasterized per size, a single-channel signed distance field (SDF), a multi-channel one (MSDF), or MTSDF, which is MSDF with a true distance in alpha. For each it covers quality across scales, the effects a material gets from the field, atlas cost, generation cost and what Go offers to generate it, and how engines do it. It then covers shaping, layout and fallback in Go beyond `x/image/font/opentype`, which v1 used. It records facts and decides nothing: the design ticket [#868](https://github.com/dvoyni/cog/issues/868) decides.

"Field pixels" are texels of the distance field. "Screen pixels" are pixels of the drawn text. *R* is the field's range: msdfgen's `-pxrange`, the width in field pixels between the least and greatest distance the field can hold, so it reaches *R*/2 on each side of the edge. *k* is the drawn em size over the field's em size.

## In short

- **Quality.** Bitmaps are the sharpest at the size they were rasterized at, and only there: magnified they blur, so each size is a set of its own. SDF holds edges at any magnification but rounds corners. MSDF keeps corners sharp: Chlumský measured up to about 40 times fewer weighted errors than a single channel at 24 to 48 field pixels, though curves gain nothing. MTSDF looks the same as MSDF. None of the three fields is hinted. Godot says this makes MSDF text less clear than rasterized text at small sizes, and x/image's bitmaps, which v1 drew, are not hinted either. Slug draws exactly from the curves at every scale. Its best case costs about 40 times a bitmap shader.
- **Effects.** A distance field gives outlines, glows, soft edges, drop shadows and dilation (bold) through a shader's parameters. A bitmap gives none of them. MSDF's median gives mitered outlines and dilation. Glows, rounded outlines and soft shadows need the true distance, which SDF and MTSDF hold. An effect reaches at most *R*/2 field pixels from the edge, minus about one screen pixel for anti-aliasing: about *k*·*R*/2 − 1 screen pixels. Godot's defaults (48 px em, *R* = 16) allow outlines up to 8 field pixels. msdf-atlas-gen's default *R* = 2 is "not intended for drawing outlines".
- **Atlas cost.** WebGPU has no three-byte format, so MSDF is stored as RGBA8, and MTSDF's alpha costs nothing more. At a 32 px em with *R* = 8, a Han glyph costs about 1.1 KB as an R8 bitmap of one size, 1.7 KB as an R8 SDF and 6.8 KB as MSDF or MTSDF. China's 3,500 common hanzi then take about 24 MB as MTSDF, one 4096² RGBA8 layer holding some 9,900 of them. At the 64 px em that thin CJK strokes ask for, they take about 71 MB. These figures are estimates from measured glyph sizes, worked in §3.
- **Generation.** A bitmap glyph costs microseconds in Go. On this machine x/image rasterized a Han glyph in 3.9 µs at 32 px. An MSDF glyph costs milliseconds: 1.9 ms at 32 px in the 2015 thesis, and 0.65 ms (eight cores) to 4.6 ms (one) at 64 px in `gogpu/gg`'s pure-Go generator. No Go generator matches msdfgen: the best two shortcut its error correction, and the rest are unfinished or engine-internal. A port of msdfgen's core is about 5,000 lines of C++ under MIT. It can skip Skia by using msdfgen's overlapping-contour combiner and scanline pass.
- **Engines.** Unity's TextMesh Pro uses single-channel SDF, generated at run time for dynamic assets in its fast SDFAA mode, and at build time with 8× to 32× oversampling for static ones. Godot draws per-size bitmaps by default, and MTSDF from msdfgen on its worker threads when a font asks for MSDF. Bevy draws only per-size bitmaps through swash, laid out by parley. `gogpu/gg` mixes them: bitmaps up to 64 px, MSDF above, and a separate 128 px MSDF atlas for CJK. MapLibre generates CJK SDFs on the client, at twice its 24 px size.
- **Shaping in Go.** `go-text/typesetting` is the only pure-Go full shaper: a port of HarfBuzz 12.3.0 with UAX #9 bidi, UAX #14 and #29 segmentation, a line wrapper and font fallback. Gio, Fyne and Ebitengine use it. Its versions are v0.x, and breaking changes bump the minor number. `x/image` shapes nothing: it kerns from GPOS pair kerning or the `kern` table, with no ligatures, bidi, line breaking or hinting. `gogpu/gg`'s own shaper covers Latin-like scripts: no mark positioning and no contextual lookups, so no Arabic or Indic.
- **What the evidence points to**, for #868 to weigh: MTSDF covers the map's "outline as a material" and continuous scaling with one set per font. Its costs are milliseconds per glyph, which a Go generator off the frame path must absorb, and four bytes per texel, which CJK multiplies. Bitmaps stay cheaper for text at a few fixed sizes, small CJK body text and pixel fonts.

## 1. Quality across scales

| | Bitmap | SDF | MSDF | MTSDF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **At its own size** | exact, as the rasterizer draws it | close; corners rounded | close | as MSDF |
| **Magnified** | blurs under bilinear filtering; needs a new rasterization per size | crisp edges; corners round off more as the field gets coarser | crisp edges and corners; curves as SDF | as MSDF |
| **Small** | best when hinted; x/image does not hint | no hinting; anti-aliasing fails once the range covers under 1 to 2 screen pixels | as SDF; thin strokes split by colouring fail first | as MSDF |
| **Thin strokes** | as the rasterizer draws them | a stroke under about two field pixels wide distorts or vanishes | as SDF | as SDF |

- **Bitmaps magnify badly.** A 64×64 coverage texture blurs under bilinear filtering and wiggles under alpha testing (Green, Fig. 1). Chlumský: raster glyphs are "only marginally faster than the distance field method, but this is negated by the need for larger image resolution" ([thesis](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/files/3050967/thesis.pdf), §1.3.1.1). Unity: bitmap fonts "produce text whose edges are more or less jagged and blurry", and its best practice for pixel art is still a bitmap mode ([Unity 6 font assets](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/UIE-font-asset.html)).
- **SDF rounds corners.** "Encoding edges using a single signed distance 'rounds off' corners as the resolution of the distance field decreases". Valve shows two channels ANDed as a fix, but kept single-channel text in Team Fortress 2 (Green, §4.3, Fig. 8).
- **MSDF fixes corners, not curves.** On the 94 printable ASCII glyphs of Open Sans, the direct MSDF method cut mismatched pixels by 3.38 times on average where the single channel was acceptable (PM < 0.01). For glyphs of straight lines only, the error fell "by several orders of magnitude" (PM at size 32: 0.00171 single, 0.00000763 direct), but "do not improve the rendering of curves". The weighted error (WPM) fell up to about 40 times at sizes 24 to 48 (32 R: 0.00522 → 0.000196). At 8 and 12 field pixels per glyph no method encoded glyphs well ([thesis](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/files/3050967/thesis.pdf), §6.2.3, Tables 6.2–6.4).
- **Thin strokes.** "If a stroke is thinner than about two cells of the distance field grid across, it cannot be encoded properly" (thesis §7.1.2). MSDF's colouring can split a thin stroke into thinner sub-strokes (§6.2.3). The light weight did worst at every size.
- **Away from the edge.** "It is designed to be most precise at glyph edges. The further away from an edge, the higher the probability that the distance is not evaluated precisely" (Chlumský in [msdfgen#67](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/issues/67)). Effects that read far from the edge meet that first. Tiny edge segments at corners also break outlines ([msdfgen#184](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/issues/184)).
- **Small sizes and range.** msdfgen: `screenPxRange()` "must never be lower than 1. If it is lower than 2, there is a high probability that the anti-aliasing will fail" ([README](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/README.md#L198)). `screenPxRange` is *R*·*k*, so a field drawn smaller than it was made needs a wider range: "if the distance field is actually 4x bigger, then you actually need a `pxrange` of 8 just for the anti-aliasing" ([msdfgen#229](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/issues/229)). The smallest em a field draws well is therefore about 2 / *R* times its own: 16 px for a 32 px field at *R* = 4, 8 px at *R* = 8, 6 px for Godot's 48 px field at *R* = 16.
- **No hinting.** Godot: MSDF fonts "at small sizes will not look as clear as rasterized fonts, due to the lack of hinting", and LCD subpixel rendering cannot be enabled for them ([Using Fonts](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/ui/gui_using_fonts.html)). v1's bitmaps were not hinted either: x/image's `sfnt` "does not support hinting", and `font.HintingFull` only rounds metrics ([sfnt.go#L630](https://github.com/golang/image/blob/v0.48.0/font/sfnt/sfnt.go#L630)). `gogpu/gg` has its own auto-hinter and TrueType bytecode interpreter in pure Go ([README](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/blob/1a73f922d77deaed0703c233ce68050de8753961/README.md#L43)).
- **Overlapping contours.** Variable fonts and fonts "converted by Google Fonts" often overlap contours. Godot's MSDF "does not render glyphs with overlapping shapes correctly" ([FontFile.xml#L649](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/doc/classes/FontFile.xml#L649)). msdfgen resolves overlaps with Skia's path ops when built with it ([resolve-shape-geometry.cpp](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/ext/resolve-shape-geometry.cpp)), and otherwise with a scanline pass that fixes the sign, the default in non-Skia builds ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/CHANGELOG.md#L25)). msdf-atlas-gen adds `-overlap`, a generator for overlapping contours ([README](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdf-atlas-gen/blob/v1.4/README.md)).
- **Slug** is exact under any transform, because it reads the curves rather than a sampled field. Lengyel's critique of the others: SDF rounds corners, and MSDF "required a complicated analysis step … and created a new class of difficult-to-avoid artifacts for complex glyphs" ([JCGT 2017](https://jcgt.org/published/0006/02/02/paper.pdf), §1).

## 2. Effects a material gets from the field

| Effect | Bitmap | SDF | MSDF (RGB median) | MTSDF (RGB + alpha) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anti-aliased edge at any scale | no | yes | yes | yes |
| Dilation, thinning (faux bold) | no | yes | yes, perpendicular | yes |
| Outline | no: a second glyph, stroked by the rasterizer | rounded | mitered (sharp) | either |
| Glow, soft edge | no | yes | not reliably | from alpha |
| Drop shadow | offset copy, hard | offset second sample, soft | hard only | soft from alpha |

- **The effects are shader functions of the distance.** Valve's shader maps the distance between two thresholds to an outline colour, or substitutes a halo below 0.5 for a glow. A drop shadow is "a second lookup into the distance field with a texture coordinate offset", and every one of them can be "dynamically controlled with shader parameters". The spread factor "also controls the domain of effect for such special rendering attributes as drop-shadows and outlines" (Green, §3, §4.2, Fig. 9). Chlumský names thickness adjustment, outline and soft shadow as transformations of the distance (thesis §5.1.3, Fig. 5.1).
- **True against perpendicular distance.** msdf-atlas-gen's table: SDF and MTSDF give *soft effects*, "that use true distance, such as glows, rounded outlines, or simplified shadows". PSDF, MSDF and MTSDF give *hard effects*, "that use perpendicular distance, such as mitered outlines or thickness adjustment". Only MTSDF has both ([README](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdf-atlas-gen/blob/v1.4/README.md#L25-L31)).
- **Godot's outline uses both.** Its canvas shader takes `d = min(median(r, g, b), a)` and clamps the outline to `px_range / 2 − 1` field pixels ([canvas.glsl#L608-L626](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/shaders/canvas.glsl#L608-L626)).
- **TextMesh Pro's effects** are material properties: Face Dilate, Outline Thickness (half inside, half outside), Underlay (offset, dilate, softness) for shadows, Glow (offset, inner, outer, power), and Bevel. Its Gradient Scale "determines the effective range of material properties such as Outline > Width and Underlay > Offset X/Y", and equals padding + 1 ([TMP 3.2 shaders](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.textmeshpro@3.2/manual/ShadersDistanceField.html)).
- **Bitmaps need a second glyph.** Godot's bitmap fonts draw outlines from glyphs FreeType strokes with `FT_Stroker`, cached per size and outline size ([text_server_adv.cpp#L1506-L1512](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/modules/text_server_adv/text_server_adv.cpp#L1506-L1512), [#L4418](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/modules/text_server_adv/text_server_adv.cpp#L4418)). v1's halo was a separate material that drew every mark a second time, on a layer beneath, under a profile fitted to painted art (`_legacy/bundles/canvas/internal/halo.go`). Its blur came from the coverage, not from a distance.
- **Slug** gets glow and drop shadow by "inflating the pixel footprint and applying a more sophisticated filter". Its bands "must be expanded to account for the largest effect radius" (JCGT §6). It names no outline.

### The widest outline for a field range

The field holds distances up to *R*/2 field pixels outside the edge. An effect's outer edge also needs about one screen pixel of distance for its own anti-aliasing (Chlumský in [msdfgen#229](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/issues/229): the outline must lie within the range "+ some extra for the anti-aliasing of the outline"). The widest outer outline is then about *k*·*R*/2 − 1 screen pixels, and an effect that must hold at every size is bounded by the smallest size drawn. Godot states the same in field pixels: "msdf_pixel_range must be set to at least twice the size of the largest font outline. The default msdf_pixel_range value of 16 allows outline sizes up to 8" ([FontFile.xml#L641](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/doc/classes/FontFile.xml#L641)). msdf-atlas-gen's default range of 2 ([main.cpp#L26](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdf-atlas-gen/blob/v1.4/msdf-atlas-gen/main.cpp#L26)) "is not intended for drawing outlines but it is sufficient for basic rendering" ([msdfgen#139](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/issues/139)). msdfgen 1.12 added an asymmetric range (`-apxrange`), which can spend more of the range outside the glyph, where outlines, glows and shadows lie, than inside ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/CHANGELOG.md#L30)).

Widest outer outline in screen pixels, by the rule above (derived, not measured):

| Field em, *R* | drawn at 16 px | 32 px | 64 px | 128 px | smallest em with `screenPxRange` ≥ 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 px, *R* = 4 (`gogpu/gg`'s default) | 0 | 1 | 3 | 7 | 16 px |
| 32 px, *R* = 8 | 1 | 3 | 7 | 15 | 8 px |
| 48 px, *R* = 16 (Godot's defaults) | 1.7 | 4.3 | 9.7 | 20.3 | 6 px |

A wider range costs atlas space: each glyph's cell grows by *R* field pixels on each axis (§3). It also spreads the field's 256 levels over more distance, so each level spans a coarser step.

## 3. Atlas cost

**Texel size.** WebGPU's 8-bit colour formats are `r8unorm` (1 byte), `rg8unorm` (2) and `rgba8unorm` (4). There is no three-byte format ([WebGPU, texture formats](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/#texture-formats)), so a three-channel MSDF takes the same 4 bytes as MTSDF, and MTSDF's alpha costs nothing more. render offers `TextureFormatR8Unorm` and `TextureFormatRGBA8Unorm` (`plugins/render/textureformat.go`). The channels are read as linear, never sRGB ([msdfgen README](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/README.md)). Bevy keeps its bitmap atlases in `Rgba8UnormSrgb`, four bytes for one channel of coverage ([font_atlas.rs#L54](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.1/crates/bevy_text/src/font_atlas.rs#L54)), as v1 stored white plus coverage in RGBA (`font.go`).

**Glyph size.** x/image's masks, measured on this machine, averaged 328 px² for printable ASCII in Noto Sans at 32 px (about 18×18), and 970 px² for the first 2,000 Han characters in Microsoft YaHei at 32 px (about 31×31). The Han average was 259 px² at 16 px and 3,759 px² at 64 px. A field cell adds *R* to each side's length, and this table adds 2 px of padding. These are estimates from those means:

| One glyph, 32 px em | Bitmap R8, one size | SDF R8, *R* = 8 | MSDF or MTSDF RGBA8, *R* = 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latin | 0.4 KB | 0.8 KB | 3.2 KB |
| Han | 1.1 KB | 1.7 KB | 6.8 KB |

**Per font.** A bitmap set is per size. Bevy keys its atlases by font, `f32` size bits, variation, hinting and smoothing ([font_atlas_set.rs#L12-L25](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.1/crates/bevy_text/src/font_atlas_set.rs#L12-L25)). An animated font size therefore grew its atlases without bound ([bevy#5636](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/5636)). Godot keys bitmaps by size and outline size, and keeps one cache at `msdf_size` for an MSDF font whatever size and outline it draws ([text_server_adv.h#L389-L407](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/modules/text_server_adv/text_server_adv.h#L389-L407)). v1 baked one face per pixel size and never reclaimed glyph pages, short of a resize that dropped them all ([render's v1 findings](https://github.com/dvoyni/cog/blob/main/plugins/render/docs/research/v1-renderers.md)). A field set is one per font, whatever sizes it is drawn at. Unity: "a resolution of 512 x 512 is fine for most fonts if you only include ASCII characters" ([Unity 6 font assets](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/UIE-font-asset.html)).

**CJK.** China's table of standard characters has 8,105 hanzi, the first level 3,500 ([Adobe CJK Type](https://ccjktype.fonts.adobe.com/2014/03/china-8105.html)). Unicode encodes 11,172 precomposed Hangul syllables ([Unicode 15.0, §3.12](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode15.0.0/ch03.pdf)). Microsoft JhengHei holds 29,386 glyphs (Slug's Table 3). Estimated totals, from the per-glyph figures above, in 10⁶ bytes:

| Han glyphs | Bitmap R8 16 px | Bitmap R8 32 px | SDF R8 32 px | MTSDF 32 px | MTSDF 64 px, *R* = 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3,500 | 1.1 MB | 3.8 MB | 5.9 MB | 23.6 MB | 71 MB |
| 8,105 | 2.7 MB | 8.9 MB | 13.7 MB | 54.8 MB | 165 MB |

One 4096² RGBA8 layer is 67 MB (64 MiB) and holds about 9,900 Han MTSDF cells at 32 px. WebGPU's default limits allow 8192² layers and 256 layers per array ([WebGPU, limits](https://www.w3.org/TR/webgpu/#limits)). Bitmaps are cheaper than one MTSDF set until several sizes, outline sizes or a continuous scale multiply them: 16, 24 and 32 px of the 3,500 hanzi together come to about 7 MB. Nobody fills these sets up front: engines fill pages as glyphs are first drawn.

- **CJK fields are made larger.** MapLibre makes client-side CJK glyphs "at 2× because CJK glyphs are more detailed than others": 48 px with a 16 px radius, against its 24 px server glyphs ([glyph_manager.ts#L54-L59](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/blob/v6.18.0/src/render/glyph_manager.ts#L54-L59), [#L443-L461](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/blob/v6.18.0/src/render/glyph_manager.ts#L443-L461)). `gogpu/gg` keeps a separate 128 px, 2048² MSDF atlas for CJK "display text (>64px)". It sends CJK at 64 px or below to bitmaps, because "No production engine uses MSDF for CJK body text" ([CHANGELOG#L1494-L1505](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/blob/1a73f922d77deaed0703c233ce68050de8753961/CHANGELOG.md#L1494-L1505)). That is the project's own claim, not a survey.
- **Engines page and fall back.** Unity: "To support thousands of characters, use large textures and enable Multi Atlas Textures. This automatically creates new textures of the same size once the main texture is full". Its dynamic assets start with an empty atlas, and for "languages with massive character sets, such as Chinese" a static asset can include only the characters needed ([Unity 6 font assets](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/UIE-font-asset.html)). Godot opens a new page of max(8 × em px, 256) texels, rounded up to a power of two, at most 2048 for MSDF and 1024 for bitmaps ([text_server_adv.cpp#L850-L857](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/modules/text_server_adv/text_server_adv.cpp#L850-L857); its sizes are in 1/64 px, [text_server_adv.h#L389-L407](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/modules/text_server_adv/text_server_adv.h#L389-L407)). At its 48 px MSDF size that page is 512².
- **Slug's CJK data.** Slug stores curves, not texels: JhengHei's 29,386 glyphs come to 52,842 KB of curve and band data (4096×507 and 4096×2350 texels), against its 20,650 KB TTF. Arial's 3,223 glyphs come to 1,549 KB (JCGT Table 3).

## 4. Generation cost, and what Go offers

| Per glyph | Source | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Bitmap, x/image, Han, 16 / 32 / 64 px | measured on this machine, one pass of 2,000 glyphs, `opentype.Face.Glyph` | 2.8 / 3.9 / 5.5 µs |
| PSDF (single channel), msdfgen, ASCII, 32 / 48 px | thesis Table 6.6, i5-3570K, 2015 | 1.7 / 3.8 ms |
| MSDF (direct), msdfgen, ASCII, 32 / 48 px | thesis Table 6.6 | 1.9 / 4.2 ms |
| MSDF, `gogpu/gg`, ASCII, 64 px, batched over goroutines | [gg#431](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/issues/431), Ryzen 7 7700X | 0.65 ms |
| MSDF, `gogpu/gg`, ASCII, 64 px, one at a time with its median filter and error correction | [gg#431](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/issues/431) | 4.6 ms |

- **The thesis figures** are for all 94 ASCII glyphs of Open Sans: 0.176 s at 32 field pixels and 0.394 s at 48 for the direct method. "All of the algorithms have linear time complexity in respect to the number of pixels", and the direct MSDF costs "only marginally higher than … a regular single channel pseudo-distance field" (thesis §6.3.1). msdfgen has since claimed "major performance improvements due to inlining" (1.11, [CHANGELOG#L50](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/CHANGELOG.md#L50)), with no figure. Every pixel also walks every edge of the shape ([ShapeDistanceFinder.hpp#L18-L24](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/core/ShapeDistanceFinder.hpp#L18-L24)), so a Han glyph of many edges costs more than an ASCII glyph of the same size.
- **On the frame path.** Two hundred new Han glyphs in one dialogue cost about 0.8 ms as 32 px bitmaps, against about 130 ms (eight cores) to 0.9 s (one) as 64 px MSDF by the `gogpu/gg` figures. That is an estimate from the rates above. Engines move the work off the frame or ahead of it. Godot generates MSDF rows on its worker thread pool ([text_server_adv.cpp#L1041-L1057](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/modules/text_server_adv/text_server_adv.cpp#L1041-L1057)) and offers "Pre-render Configurations" to avoid stutter. It also notes that new MSDF glyphs "at small font sizes may be more expensive", while there is less stuttering for large sizes ([Using Fonts](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/ui/gui_using_fonts.html)). Unity makes SDFAA "the default mode for Dynamic or Dynamic OS font assets for fast generation" ([Unity 6 font assets](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/UIE-font-asset.html)). The thesis expects run-time generation "when the character set is too large, and only an unknown small portion is expected to be used" (§6.3).
- **Baking at build time** fits known text. msdf-atlas-gen writes an atlas image and its layout as JSON, CSV or an Artery Font file from a charset file ([README](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdf-atlas-gen/blob/v1.4/README.md)). Its CLI defaults are a 32 px em and a range of 2 ([main.cpp#L23-L26](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdf-atlas-gen/blob/v1.4/msdf-atlas-gen/main.cpp#L23-L26)). It is C++ built with vcpkg against FreeType, so the asset pipeline would shell out to it. Baking cannot cover user input, player names or a language's full set. Unity's best practice for "User input" is a dynamic asset in SDFAA, and its static assets use SDF16 or SDF32 ([Unity 6 font assets](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/UIE-font-asset.html)). Godot's prerendered glyphs sit beside glyphs "rasterized on-the-fly as usual".

None is a full port of msdfgen. Writing one means porting msdfgen's `core`: 6,688 lines of C++, of which about 1,500 save files, render, describe shapes and estimate error, leaving about 5,000 for shapes, edge colouring, distance selection, generation, the scanline pass and error correction ([core](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/tree/v1.13.1/core)). It has no dependencies ([README](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdfgen/blob/v1.13.1/README.md)) and is MIT. Outlines come pure-Go from go-text's `font.GlyphOutline` ([renderer.go](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/font/renderer.go#L37-L43)) or x/image's `sfnt.LoadGlyph`.

A single-channel SDF needs no port. MapLibre's TinySDF rasterizes with Canvas 2D and runs the Felzenszwalb–Huttenlocher distance transform ([README](https://github.com/mapbox/tiny-sdf)). Valve brute-forced a small neighbourhood of a high-resolution binary image, whose "execution time … is negligible" (Green, §3). In Go, `x/image/vector` rasterizes, and the transform is written by hand.

## 5. How engines and libraries do it

| | Representation | Generation | Atlas | Shaping |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unity TextMesh Pro / TextCore** | single-channel SDF, or bitmap modes (SMOOTH, RASTER, hinted variants, COLOR) | FreeType; SDFAA "faster, but less accurate", SDF8/16/32 oversampled; static (build), dynamic and dynamic OS (run time) | per font asset, Multi Atlas Textures, fallback asset lists | TMP 3.2: kerning pairs only from the `kern` table, since FreeType reads no GPOS |
| **Godot 4.7** | per-size bitmaps by default; MTSDF when `multichannel_signed_distance_field` | FreeType outlines into msdfgen's `MultiAndTrueDistance` on worker threads, error-corrected, RGBA8 | shelf-packed pages, 256² to 2048² | HarfBuzz in `TextServerAdvanced` |
| **Bevy 0.20** | per-size bitmaps, RGBA8 sRGB | swash, on demand | ≥ 512², power of two, 2 px padding | parley 0.11 (HarfRust, ICU4X segmentation) |
| **msdfgen + msdf-atlas-gen** | hard mask, soft mask, SDF, PSDF, MSDF, MTSDF | offline CLI, or `DynamicAtlas` at run time | tight bin packing or uniform grid | none; exports kerning |
| **Slug** | Bézier curves and bands in textures | offline preprocessing | RGBA16F curve texture, RG16Uint band texture | outside Slug |
| **`gogpu/gg`** | bitmap ≤ 64 px, MSDF above; CJK MSDF at 128 px | Go, on demand | 1024² Latin, 2048² CJK | own GSUB/GPOS subset |
| **MapLibre** | SDF | server glyphs at 24 px; CJK on the client with TinySDF at 48 px | per font stack | its own; a grapheme cluster of a complex script rasterized whole on the client |

- **Unity.** Render modes and the GPOS note: [Font Asset Creator, TMP 3.2](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.textmeshpro@3.2/manual/FontAssetsCreator.html). Population modes, multi atlas, "SDF32: Default mode for Static font assets", and "SDF16 … for … titles that are 72 points in size or larger": [Unity 6 font assets](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/UIE-font-asset.html). Fallbacks: "searching the list for missing characters requires extra computing resources", and extra fonts take extra draw calls ([TMP 3.2 font asset properties](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.textmeshpro@3.2/manual/FontAssetsProperties.html)).
- **Godot.** `msdf_pixel_range` defaults to 16 and `msdf_size` to 48 ([FontFile.xml#L640-L645](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/doc/classes/FontFile.xml#L640-L645)). It generates MTSDF, not MSDF: the generator is `_generateMTSDF_threaded`, into a `Bitmap<float, 4>` ([text_server_adv.cpp#L970-L1069](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/modules/text_server_adv/text_server_adv.cpp#L970-L1069)). Godot uses the alpha for outlines (§2).
- **Bevy.** Glyphs render through `swash::scale::Render` from colour outlines, colour bitmaps or outlines into `Format::Alpha` ([font_atlas.rs#L200-L207](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.1/crates/bevy_text/src/font_atlas.rs#L200-L207)). A new atlas is the smallest power of two that fits the glyph, at least 512 ([#L160-L171](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.1/crates/bevy_text/src/font_atlas.rs#L160-L171)). `bevy_text` depends on parley and swash ([Cargo.toml](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.1/crates/bevy_text/Cargo.toml)). Parley depends on HarfRust, skrifa and ICU4X's segmenter ([parley Cargo.toml](https://github.com/linebender/parley/blob/v0.11.0/parley/Cargo.toml)). No distance-field text is in `bevy_text`.
- **msdf-atlas-gen.** `DynamicAtlas` adds glyphs "on-the-fly". Its example's `ImmediateAtlasGenerator` "isn't ideal for this purpose because it may launch new threads every time", so a real one brings its own generator ([README](https://github.com/Chlumsky/msdf-atlas-gen/blob/v1.4/README.md)).
- **Slug.** On a GeForce GTX 1060, a two-megapixel area of 50 lines at 32 px per em took 1.1 ms in Arial (about 20 curves per capital), 1.3 ms in Centaur (48), 3.8 ms in Halloween (72) and 13.3 ms in Wildwood (546). In the best case "a conventional shader sampling prerendered glyph images (without an SDF) requires 26 µs", about 40 times less (JCGT §5, Table 2). Curves cost "slightly larger than eight bytes per curve" (§4). The 2026 shaders dropped the band split optimisation and adaptive supersampling, and draw multicolour glyphs as stacked glyphs ([*A Decade of Slug*](https://terathon.com/blog/decade-slug.html)). Lengyel dedicated patent US 10,373,352 to the public domain effective 2026-03-17. The reference HLSL shaders are MIT or Apache-2.0, and distributing them requires credit ([EricLengyel/Slug](https://github.com/EricLengyel/Slug)). A WGSL port would be ours. Both texture formats exist in WebGPU.
- **`gogpu/gg`.** Text modes, the 64 px switch from MSDF to bitmaps, and the CJK atlases: [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/blob/1a73f922d77deaed0703c233ce68050de8753961/CHANGELOG.md#L428-L436) and [ADR-027's entry](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/blob/1a73f922d77deaed0703c233ce68050de8753961/CHANGELOG.md#L1494-L1505).
- **MapLibre.** `localIdeographFontFamily` overrides "generation of Chinese, Japanese, and Korean characters" locally, to "avoid bandwidth-intensive glyph server requests" ([MapOptions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/API/type-aliases/MapOptions/)).
- **Go toolkits** draw bitmaps. Ebitengine's `text/v2` rasterizes go-text outlines per size and subpixel offset ([gotext.go#L385-L420](https://github.com/hajimehoshi/ebiten/blob/v2.10.6/text/v2/gotext.go#L385-L420)).

## 6. Shaping, layout and fallback in Go

| Package | Shaping | Bidi | Line breaking | Fallback | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `golang.org/x/image/font/opentype`, `sfnt` v0.48.0 | none: cmap, advances, kerning from GPOS pair kerning or `kern` | no | no | no | stable, Go team; "not hardened against malicious inputs" |
| `github.com/go-text/typesetting` v0.3.5 | HarfBuzz port (GSUB, GPOS, AAT, Arabic, Indic, USE, Khmer, Myanmar, Thai, Hangul, Hebrew) | UAX #9 | UAX #14, grapheme UAX #29, `LineWrapper` | `fontscan.FontMap`: system fonts, CSS-like family substitution, per-rune resolution | v0.x with breaking minor bumps; Unlicense or BSD-3-Clause; used by Gio, Fyne, Ebitengine |
| `github.com/gogpu/gg/text` | own: GSUB 1–4 and 7, GPOS 1, 2 and 9, `kern` | claimed in README | own `wrap.go` | own | MIT, pre-1.0, ships often |
| `golang.org/x/text/unicode/bidi` | — | UAX #9, "UNDER CONSTRUCTION" | — | — | experimental |
| `github.com/benoitkugler/textlayout` | HarfBuzz and Graphite port | — | — | — | superseded by go-text |

- **x/image.** `opentype` rasterizes through `x/image/vector` ([opentype.go](https://github.com/golang/image/blob/v0.48.0/font/opentype/opentype.go#L1-L20)). `sfnt.Font.Kern` reads GPOS kern lookups when present ([sfnt.go#L1663-L1666](https://github.com/golang/image/blob/v0.48.0/font/sfnt/sfnt.go#L1663-L1666)). It parses no GSUB, so there are no ligatures or contextual forms. v1 laid text out rune by rune: each rune's advance, plus kerning only between runes drawn by the same font, along a fallback chain picked per language (`font.go`, `MeasureLine`, `pairKern`). It had no bidi, no complex-script shaping and no UAX #14 line breaking.
- **go-text.** Its `harfbuzz` package "is a direct port of the C/C++ library", "based on upstream commit 5a31dd0 (v12.3.0)" ([harfbuzz.go#L1-L24](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/harfbuzz/harfbuzz.go#L1-L24)). Its `shaping.Segmenter` splits input by bidi direction, script, language, vertical orientation and face ([input.go](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/shaping/input.go)). `shaping.LineWrapper` breaks on UAX #14 boundaries and falls back to grapheme boundaries inside a word ([wrapping.go](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/shaping/wrapping.go)). `fontscan.FontMap` "supports system and user-provided fonts, and implements the CSS font substitutions". It is "NOT safe for concurrent use", and its first system scan is "rather long", cached on disk ([fontmap.go](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/fontscan/fontmap.go)). The `font` package reads outlines, bitmaps, SVG and COLR glyphs and variable fonts. The README: "although already used in production by UI toolkits, still evolves rapidly … breaking changes will bump the minor version number" ([README](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/README.md)). The licence is "Unlicense OR BSD-3-Clause" ([LICENSE](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/LICENSE)). Its only non-test dependency is `x/image` ([go.mod](https://github.com/go-text/typesetting/blob/v0.3.5/go.mod)). Ebitengine v2.10.6, Gio and Fyne all require v0.3.5. v1's language-keyed fallback chain fits `shaping.Fontmap`, an interface with one method, `ResolveFace(rune)`, which 2D can implement itself instead of taking `fontscan`'s system scan.
- **gogpu/gg.** `OwnShaper` lists GSUB single, multiple, alternate, ligature and extension lookups, and GPOS single, pair and extension ([shaper_own.go](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/blob/1a73f922d77deaed0703c233ce68050de8753961/text/shaper_own.go#L1-L23)). Without GPOS mark attachment or contextual lookups, Arabic joining and Indic reordering cannot shape. Its script table says "complex scripts will need more detailed mapping in the future" ([#L512-L514](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/blob/1a73f922d77deaed0703c233ce68050de8753961/text/shaper_own.go#L512-L514)). Its README claims it is "5-10x faster than HarfBuzz" ([README#L43](https://github.com/gogpu/gg/blob/1a73f922d77deaed0703c233ce68050de8753961/README.md#L43)), unmeasured here.
- **Cost.** Neither go-text nor its users publish shaping benchmarks or binary-size figures that were found, and none were measured here. HarfBuzz-style shaping is per run, so engines shape a string once and cache the glyphs, as gg's "shape-once" scene text does.

## Not verified

- **Measurements.** The bitmap timings are one pass on this development machine, with x/image v0.48.0 and Go 1.27.4, not a benchmark with repetitions. The 2,000 Han characters are the first the font maps from U+4E00, not a frequency list. Running the Go MSDF generators here was not permitted, so their speed and quality rest on the gg#431 reporter's figures (gg v0.50.4, before the lock fix) and on reading their code. `christerso/msdf`'s and `moozd/msdf`'s output was never checked. msdfgen's own speed is known only from the thesis's 2015 figures.
- **Derived figures.** The atlas sizes in §3, the outline table in §2 and the frame-path estimate in §4 are arithmetic on the cited figures and the measured mask sizes, with 2 px padding and square cells. Real packing wastes space ([#863](https://github.com/dvoyni/cog/issues/863) covers packing). The rule "*k*·*R*/2 − 1" takes the one-pixel anti-aliasing margin from msdfgen#229's description, not from a measurement.
- **CJK field size.** That a 32 px field is too coarse for Han rests on the thesis's two-cell stroke limit and on the 2× choices of MapLibre and gogpu/gg. No CJK stroke widths were measured, and no source tested MSDF error on Han glyphs.
- **Unity** is closed source: SDFAA's algorithm, how a dynamic atlas generates glyphs and the current TextCore's GPOS support were not read. The commonly cited 1:10 padding-to-size ratio is a community rule, not in Unity's manuals.
- **Slug's numbers** are from 2017 hardware and the 2017 shader, whose band split and supersampling the 2026 shaders dropped.
- **go-text** was read at its API and comments. Its HarfBuzz conformance and performance against C HarfBuzz were not tested. Whether `sushvibez/gfx` or `derekmwright/glyphengine` hold better generators was not checked.
- **Japanese counts.** The jōyō kanji count (2,136) and JIS levels were not confirmed against a primary source, and are left out.
